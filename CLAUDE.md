This is **Jordan's** personal Obsidian vault — a single-owner knowledge and planning vault. Jordan is the **sole interested party**: every note exists to serve *their* memory, planning, and dependencies. It is not a shared team wiki or a general tracker for other people's work.

## The vault-owner principle

When creating or grooming notes, ask "does this matter to the owner?" before recording it. In particular:

- **Person notes' `## Open loops` are owner-centric.** A loop belongs on someone's note only when the owner has a genuine **touchpoint or dependency** — it's part of a project the owner works on, or the owner is waiting on / blocked by / feeding into it. Do **not** turn a person note into a task list of everything that person owns. A commitment someone else made (e.g. in an all-hands) that the owner has no stake in stays in the source meeting note's `## Commitments`; it does not get copied onto the person note or the daily note.
- **People can still be created as reference context** even without an owner-relevant loop — just leave their `## Open loops` empty rather than inventing one.
- **Record the owner's active work areas here** (projects, domains, recurring vendors/partners) so relevance calls are grounded. When unsure whether something is relevant, prefer leaving it off and note it in the source meeting note instead.
  - _e.g._ list the projects and domains the owner actually works on

## Structure

Numbered folders: `1. People`, `2. Projects`, `3. Decisions`, `4. Companies`, `5. Meetings`, `6. Daily`, `7. Knowledge`, `9. Sources`, plus `0. Templates` and per-category `... MOC.md` index notes. Templates in `0. Templates/` are authoritative — always read the relevant one before creating a note, and follow its frontmatter keys and `##` headings exactly. Links use `[[Wikilink]]` style. See [[schema]] for the full model.

The `daily-summary` and `vault-groom` skills operate on this vault and already encode this owner-centric behavior.
